## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned**

Building became cheap, so deciding what to build became the real work. After the hospital session
on 17 Nov, AI helped our team turn the hospital's requests into a working v2 quickly, but it could
not tell us which request mattered most. That was my job as Product Owner. In Sprint 0 I cut the
MUST list down to one path a user can finish, and every backlog item still had to trace back to a
real pain source (INT-01, DOC-01, DOC-02). AI also writes in a confident voice even when it is
wrong, so I learned to check its output against the requirement, not against how finished it looks.

**2. Where I stand**

I think I am at the beginning of the AI era, not ahead of it. I can direct AI to produce code and
documents for a problem I understand, and I can judge whether the result answers the hospital's
requests (F-02, F-03, F-06). I cannot yet judge the code underneath as well as a senior engineer,
so I rely on tests and on my teammates to catch what I miss. My strength is the question before the
code: who has this pain, and what is the smallest thing that fixes it. AI still cannot decide that
for me.

**3. My plan**

Next year I want to practise this on one more real problem with a real user, and write the
requirement down the same way: a source, a priority, and a clear "won't do". I also want to read
code well enough to verify what AI produces. In five years I hope to work on a product where I help
decide what gets built, but I do not know the exact role yet. Whatever it is, I will ask the user
first and use AI to build faster, not to decide for me.
