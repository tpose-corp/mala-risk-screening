## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned**

AI moved the hard part of software engineering. Writing code and documents became fast. After the
hospital session on 17 Nov, our team turned 11 change requests into a working v2 before the final
presentation, which would not have been possible without AI. Our `CLAUDE.md` rules also kept AI
away from the clinical logic, so speed did not cost patient safety.

What got worse is that checking became the bottleneck. AI output looks finished even when it is
not. One feature I thought was done had missed the audit rules from the Computer Crime Act §26,
and I only found it when I read `rule.md` line by line against the code. My lesson: when building
is cheap, understanding the requirement and proving the result are the real work.

**2. Where I stand**

Honestly, I do not think I have stepped into the industry yet. I solved the problems in front of
me and competed only with those problems, not with anyone in the industry. It feels like walking
through fog. No one told me exactly what to do, so I did what was assigned, as well as I could see
it. I am not sure I chose the best way. AI makes the fog strange: it answers anything I ask, but it
cannot tell me what I should be asking. I learned many new things this term, but I still do not
know what I do not know. What the real industry looks like is still a question for me.

**3. My plan**

I will keep moving forward and adapt to whatever I meet. Next year, I will have finished my
internship and may start my first job. I will try everything I face. I will fail, I will cry, and
then I will succeed and be happy with it. I do not know how far I can go in five years.
But looking back at where I started in this course, I have already come a long way.
