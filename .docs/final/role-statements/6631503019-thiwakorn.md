## Reflection

**1. Lessons learned**

One important lesson I learned from this project is that using AI does not mean we can stop thinking for ourselves. AI can help write code, explain problems, and speed up development, but it does not always understand the real requirements. Since our project is related to patient risk screening, we cannot simply trust every answer AI gives us. I learned that setting clear rules before using AI is important. Writing CLAUDE.md helped our team define what AI could and could not do, especially keeping clinical decisions under human responsibility. I also learned that being responsible for AI is not only about using it effectively, but also about checking its output and being honest about how it was used.

**2. Where I stand in the AI era**

I think I am still learning how to work effectively in an AI-driven world. I can use AI tools to help with programming, debugging, and understanding unfamiliar topics, but I do not think I am an expert yet. Sometimes I know what to ask AI, but sometimes I still struggle to identify the actual problem. I see AI as a tool that makes learning and development faster, not something that can replace my own understanding. I still need to improve my technical skills and learn how to evaluate AI-generated results instead of accepting them immediately.

**3. My plan**

Next year, I plan to gain real working experience through my internship and improve my skills in software development, especially backend development and system design. I also want to become better at using AI tools responsibly in real projects. In five years, I hope to become a capable software engineer who can develop reliable systems, understand the problems behind the requirements, and make good technical decisions. I do not expect to know everything, but I want to keep learning and become someone who can take responsibility for the work I produce.
